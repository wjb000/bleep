Open-source desk droid

Bleep walks, looks around and beeps back.

A 34 cm two-legged droid you can print in a weekend. It has 11 smart servos, an ESP32-S3 brain with a camera eye, and a round LCD face. It learned to walk in MuJoCo, and the same network runs on the robot.

Download & buildSource on GitHub

What it does out of the box

Walks and turns

The walking policy is a 23k-parameter network trained with PPO, running at 50 Hz on the ESP32-S3. Steer it from your phone over its own Wi-Fi, or tap its dome to start and stop.

Trained in sim

Looks around

A camera sits beside the eye. The dome turns to follow movement it sees (up to ±86° while tracking; the joint itself turns ±120°).

In firmware

Hears and feels

A microphone and a copper touch pad inside the dome. A loud sound makes it curious; a pat makes it happy.

In firmwareLoudness only, no words yet

Has a face

A 1.28" round LCD eye blinks and changes mood (idle, happy, curious, sleepy, sad, low battery), with matching chirps from a piezo buzzer.

In firmware

Knows when it fell

The IMU detects a fall: the legs go limp to protect the servos, the face turns sad, and it stands back up into its crouch once you set it upright.

In firmwareSelf get-up: not trained yet

Hackable

Every part is parametric CadQuery. The URDF and MuJoCo model come from the same CAD, so if you change a dimension you can retrain.

Training curve

Walking policy in sim

The pipeline

  1. One CAD source. cad/bleep.py builds the printed parts. The same script writes the URDF, the MJCF, the masses and inertias (PLA at 70% fill, servos 58 g each) and this 3D model.
  2. Servo model, not ideal motors. sim/servo.py models the STS3215's P-controller with a speed-dependent torque limit, plus joint friction, damping and rotor inertia.
  3. Domain randomisation. Training varies everything a real build varies: friction 0.5-1.2, link mass ±10%, body COM ±1 cm, servo gain ±20%, stall torque 85-110%, zero offsets ±0.03 rad, joint damping and friction, 0-1 step of latency, sensor noise, and shoves every 2-5 s.
  4. System ID. sim/sysid.py records a servo swinging a weighted arm and fits gain, damping, friction and stall torque, so the randomisation is centred on your servos.
  5. Bit-exact deployment. The network is exported to C. The firmware's C forward pass is checked against PyTorch on random inputs.
